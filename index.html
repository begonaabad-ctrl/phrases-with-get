Expressions with get

get hold of someone
manage to contact or speak to someone.
get into trouble with someone
cause a problem or get into a difficult situation with a person or authority.
get out of the way
move so that you are no longer blocking someone or something.
get your own back on someone
take revenge on someone who has treated you badly.
get rid of something/someone
make something or someone unwanted go away; dispose of.
get the chance
have an opportunity to do something.
get the impression
form an opinion or feeling based on what you notice.
get the joke
understand why something is funny.
get the wrong end of the stick
misunderstand what someone means or what has happened.
get to know someone
gradually become familiar with someone.

Idioms with get

Get real!
Be sensible and recognise the facts; stop being unrealistic.
Get a life!
Stop being boring or obsessing over something unimportant; do something more worthwhile.
not get anywhere
make no progress.
get on someone's nerves
annoy or irritate someone.
get your act together
organise yourself and start behaving or working effectively.
get on like a house on fire
like each other immediately and have a very good relationship.
get a move on
hurry up.
be getting on a bit
be rather old.
get the message
understand what someone is trying to tell you, especially indirectly.
get your own way
get what you want, often by persuading others.

Phrasal verbs with get

get together
start a romantic relationship; or meet socially, depending on context.
get over something
recover from an illness, shock or emotional difficulty.
get on with something
continue doing something, especially after a pause.
get through to someone
make someone understand or accept what you are saying.
get into something
enter or begin a career, profession or activity.
get around
travel or move from place to place.
get away with something
do something wrong without being caught or punished.
get by
manage with the money, time or resources available.
get behind
fail to make enough progress; fall behind schedule.
get someone down
make someone feel unhappy or depressed.
get out of something
avoid a responsibility or obligation.
get back to someone
reply to someone by speaking or writing later.
